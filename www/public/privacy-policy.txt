PHANTOM-WG PRIVACY POLICY

Effective date: 29 September 2026
Last updated: 29 September 2026

Türkçe: https://www.phantom.tc/privacy-policy-tr.txt

Phantom-WG is a WireGuard VPN client for iOS, published by ARTEK İnovasyon
Ar-Ge Sanayi ve Ticaret Limited Şirketi. This policy describes what the app
does with your information. It covers the app itself. It does not cover the
servers you connect to, because you run those, not us.


WE DO NOT COLLECT YOUR DATA

Phantom-WG contains no analytics, no telemetry, no advertising and no
third-party tracking library. The app makes no network request of its own:
no update check, no crash upload, nothing, so no usage, crash or device
information reaches us from it. The only connection it opens is the tunnel
written in the configuration you imported.

There is no account to create and no sign-in. We do not know that you have
installed the app, when you use it, or where you connect.

The app does contain links to our website. Following one opens your browser,
and our website then sees an ordinary web request the way any website does.
That is a visit you started, not something the app sends on its own, and it
is covered by the website's own policy rather than this one.


WHAT STAYS ON YOUR DEVICE

Everything the app saves, it saves on your device:

  - Secrets (your WireGuard private key, any pre-shared key, and the
    ghost-mode secret) are written to the iOS Keychain, in an access group
    shared only by Phantom-WG and its own network extension. We never
    receive them.

  - The tunnel's name and the server address it points at are not in the
    Keychain. They live in the VPN profile the app creates in iOS, and are
    visible to anyone holding your unlocked device under Settings >
    General > VPN & Device Management.

  - Your interface language, if you change it with the language button, is
    saved as a single app setting. Until you change it, nothing is written.

The app keeps no database and writes no documents of its own; the three
items above are the whole of what it stores.

Deleting a tunnel deletes its Keychain entry and its VPN profile. Deleting
the app removes what remains.

One thing to know about backups: the Keychain entries are written so that
the extension can reconnect after a restart, which also means iOS includes
them in an encrypted device backup if you have backups switched on. They
travel inside Apple's backup of your device, under your Apple Account, never
to us.


THE SERVERS YOU CONNECT TO

Phantom-WG connects to the endpoint written in the configuration you import.
In ghost mode it also carries your WireGuard traffic inside a WebSocket
connection to the bridge named in that same configuration. We operate
neither. Phantom is self-hosted: the server is one you provisioned and
installed yourself.

What those servers record about your connection, if they record anything, is
a matter between you and whoever runs them.

DNS. If the configuration you import does not carry a DNS line, the app
fills one in for you with Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Quad9 (9.9.9.9). The
values it filled in are shown on the tunnel's detail screen, and you can
replace them by editing the configuration. While the tunnel is up, those
resolvers see the domain names your device looks up. In ghost mode there is
also one query before the tunnel exists: the bridge's hostname is resolved
by whatever resolver your device is already using, over the ordinary network
connection, so that the app knows where to open the carrier.

Between your device and the endpoint, your traffic is encrypted by
WireGuard. That encryption holds against us as well: we are not a hop on
that path, and the ghost-mode relay that is a hop is one of your own
servers.


WHAT THE APP DOES ON ITS OWN

Starting a tunnel does more than start it once. The app asks iOS to bring
that tunnel back by itself whenever a network is available: after the app
is closed, after iOS terminates it in the background, and after the device
restarts. This is why starting a tunnel is treated as saying you want it
running.

Turning the tunnel off withdraws that arrangement in the same step. If iOS
refuses to record the withdrawal, the app stops the tunnel anyway and tells
you on the tunnel's screen that the automatic reconnection could not be
taken down.


WHAT TRAVELS OUTSIDE THE TUNNEL

A VPN carries what it is configured to carry, and this one is no exception:

  - The AllowedIPs value in your configuration decides which traffic enters
    the tunnel. A narrow value means the rest of your traffic does not go
    through it. The app refuses a configuration with no AllowedIPs at all,
    but it does not force a full tunnel on you.

  - In ghost mode the bridge's own addresses are deliberately routed
    outside the tunnel. They have to be: the connection that carries the
    tunnel cannot be carried by it.

  - There is no kill switch. If the tunnel drops, traffic goes over your
    ordinary network connection until it is back.


THE CLIPBOARD

Two buttons in the app put things on the iOS system clipboard, which is
shared with other apps and, if Handoff is on, with your other Apple devices:

  - "Copy Config" copies the tunnel's configuration as plain text. That
    text contains your private key and any pre-shared key.

  - "Copy Logs" copies the diagnostic log described below.

Both are yours to press; nothing is copied unless you press them. The import
screen's "Paste" button reads the clipboard so it can fill the configuration
field, and iOS may ask you to allow that read.


LOGS

While a tunnel is running, the app keeps a short diagnostic log so you can
see what the connection is doing. That log:

  - is held in memory by the network extension and is never written to disk
    by the app;

  - holds a limited number of recent lines, and is emptied each time a
    tunnel starts;

  - stops being shown once the tunnel is not running, and cannot outlive
    the extension's own memory;

  - is never transmitted anywhere.

If you copy it to show someone, read it first: it describes your connection,
including the address of the bridge you connect to.


CAMERA

If you import a configuration by scanning a QR code, the app uses the camera
to read that code and for nothing else. No photo or video is recorded, kept
or transmitted, and scanning stops the moment a code is read. You may
decline camera access; the app will say so and you can paste the
configuration instead.


INFORMATION APPLE MAY GIVE US

Depending on what you have chosen to share with developers in your device
settings, Apple may give us aggregate usage statistics and crash reports for
the app. That information comes from Apple rather than from the app, reaches
us in a form that does not identify you, and is not combined with anything
else. You control it under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics &
Improvements.
