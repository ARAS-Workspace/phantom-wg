PHANTOM-WG PRIVACY POLICY

Effective date: 29 September 2026
Last updated: 23 November 2026

Türkçe: https://www.phantom.tc/privacy-policy-tr.txt

Phantom-WG is a WireGuard VPN client for iOS, published by ARTEK İnovasyon
Ar-Ge Sanayi ve Ticaret Limited Şirketi. This policy describes what the app
does with your information. It covers the app itself. It does not cover the
servers you connect to, because we do not run them.

This text describes version 1.3.0 of the app. Where an earlier version
behaved differently in a way that still matters to you, it says so.


WE DO NOT COLLECT YOUR DATA

Phantom-WG contains no analytics, no telemetry, no advertising and no
third-party tracking library. The app makes no network request of its own:
no update check, no crash upload, nothing, so no usage, crash or device
information reaches us from it. The only connection it opens is the tunnel
written in the configuration you imported.

There is no account to create and no sign-in. We do not know that you have
installed the app, when you use it, or where you connect.

The app does contain links to our website. Following one opens your browser,
and our website then sees an ordinary web request the way any website does.
That is a visit you started, not something the app sends on its own, and it
is covered by the website's own policy rather than this one.


WHAT STAYS ON YOUR DEVICE

Everything the app saves, it saves on your device:

  - Each tunnel's configuration, secrets included (your WireGuard private
    key, any pre-shared key, and the ghost-mode secret), is written to the
    iOS Keychain as one item. The item sits in an access group shared only
    by Phantom-WG and its own network extension. We never receive it.

  - The tunnel's name and the server address it points at are also written
    to the VPN profile the app creates in iOS. There they are visible to
    anyone holding your unlocked device under Settings > General > VPN &
    Device Management.

  - Your interface language, if you change it with the language button, is
    saved as a single app setting. Until you change it, nothing is written.

  - The order of your tunnel list, if you rearrange it, is saved as an app
    setting. It holds the tunnels' internal identifiers and nothing else.

The app keeps no database. It writes a file only when you export, as
described under FILES below. The four items above are the whole of what it
stores.

Deleting a tunnel deletes its Keychain entry and its VPN profile. Each time
it starts, the app also deletes any Keychain entry that no tunnel refers
to.

Deleting the app removes its VPN profiles and its settings. iOS may leave
the Keychain entries on the device. No other app can read them. If you
install Phantom-WG again, it deletes them the first time it starts.

One thing to know about backups: the Keychain entries are written so that
the extension can reconnect after a restart, which also means iOS includes
them in an encrypted device backup if you have backups switched on. They
travel inside Apple's backup of your device, under your Apple Account, never
to us.


THE SERVERS YOU CONNECT TO

Phantom-WG connects to the endpoint written in the configuration you import.
In ghost mode it also carries your WireGuard traffic inside a WebSocket
connection to the bridge named in that same configuration. We operate
neither. The servers are the ones written in your configuration: your own,
or someone else's that you chose to trust.

What those servers record about your connection, if they record anything, is
a matter between you and whoever runs them.

DNS. The app adds no DNS server of its own. If a tunnel's configuration
carries a DNS line, the tunnel uses the resolvers written there, and while
the tunnel is up they see the domain names your device looks up. If it
carries none, the app sets no resolver for the tunnel and your device keeps
using the ones it already has.

Versions before 1.3.0 did this differently: a configuration without a DNS
line was filled in with Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Quad9 (9.9.9.9). A tunnel
you imported with one of those versions keeps the values it was given. They
are shown on the tunnel's detail screen. You can replace or remove them by
editing the configuration.

There is also one query before the tunnel exists. If the configuration
names its server by hostname (the endpoint, or in ghost mode the bridge),
that name is resolved by whatever resolver your device is already using,
over the ordinary network connection, so that the app knows where to
connect.

Between your device and the endpoint, your traffic is encrypted by
WireGuard. That encryption holds against us as well: we are not a hop on
that path. The ghost-mode relay that is a hop is the bridge named in your
configuration.


WHAT THE APP DOES ON ITS OWN

Starting a tunnel does more than start it once. The app asks iOS to bring
that tunnel back by itself whenever a network is available: after the app
is closed, after iOS terminates it in the background, and after the device
restarts. This is why starting a tunnel is treated as saying you want it
running.

Turning the tunnel off withdraws that arrangement in the same step. If iOS
refuses to record the withdrawal, or does not answer within three seconds,
the app stops the tunnel anyway. It tells you on the tunnel's screen that
the automatic reconnection could not be taken down, or that its removal was
not confirmed. In that case iOS may bring the tunnel back by itself.


WHAT TRAVELS OUTSIDE THE TUNNEL

A VPN carries what it is configured to carry, and this one is no exception:

  - The AllowedIPs value in your configuration decides which traffic enters
    the tunnel. A narrow value means the rest of your traffic does not go
    through it. The app refuses a configuration with no AllowedIPs at all,
    but it does not force a full tunnel on you.

  - In ghost mode the bridge's own addresses are deliberately routed
    outside the tunnel. They have to be: the connection that carries the
    tunnel cannot be carried by it.

  - There is no kill switch. If the tunnel drops, traffic goes over your
    ordinary network connection until it is back.


THE CLIPBOARD

Two buttons in the app put things on the iOS system clipboard, which other
apps on your device can read:

  - "Copy Config (.conf)" copies the tunnel's configuration as plain text.
    That text contains your private key, any pre-shared key and the
    ghost-mode secret. The app first asks iOS to confirm the device owner
    (see below) and marks the copy as local to this device, so Handoff
    does not pass it to your other Apple devices. Versions before 1.3.0
    did neither.

  - "Copy Logs" copies the diagnostic log described below. This is an
    ordinary copy: if Handoff is on, it reaches your other Apple devices
    too.

Both are yours to press. Nothing is copied unless you press them. The import
screen's "Paste from Clipboard" button reads the clipboard so it can fill
the configuration field, and iOS may ask you to allow that read.


FACE ID, TOUCH ID AND THE PASSCODE

Before a configuration is copied, exported, edited or deleted, the app asks
iOS to confirm that the person holding the device is its owner: with Face
ID, Touch ID or the device passcode. iOS does the checking and tells the app
only whether it succeeded. The app never sees your face or fingerprint data
or your passcode, and stores nothing about the check. On a device with no
passcode there is nothing to ask, and these actions go ahead.


FILES

Importing reads the .conf files and zip archives you pick in the system
file picker, and nothing else. The app keeps no copy of those files. What it
takes from a file is the configuration, which is stored as described above.

Exporting writes a file that holds your private key and the other secrets
in plain text: a .conf file for one tunnel, or one zip archive for all of
them. iOS confirms the device owner first. The app prepares the file in its
temporary folder, and the system file picker moves it to the place you
choose. The temporary folder is removed whether you save or cancel. A file
left behind by an interrupted export is removed the next time the app
starts. Once the file is in the place you chose, it is yours to look after:
where you put it (iCloud Drive, another app, another device) decides who
else can read it. The app does not encrypt it.


LOGS

While a tunnel is running, the app keeps a short diagnostic log so you can
see what the connection is doing. That log:

  - is held in memory by the network extension and is never written to disk
    by the app;

  - holds at most 2,000 lines. When it is full it is emptied and starts
    again. It is also emptied each time a tunnel starts and when you press
    "Clear";

  - stops being shown once the tunnel is not running, and cannot outlive
    the extension's own memory;

  - is never transmitted anywhere.

If you copy it to show someone, read it first: it describes your connection,
including the address of the bridge you connect to.


CAMERA

If you import a configuration by scanning a QR code, the app uses the camera
to read that code and for nothing else. No photo or video is recorded, kept
or transmitted, and scanning stops the moment a code is read. You may
decline camera access; the app will say so and you can paste the
configuration or import a file instead.


INFORMATION APPLE MAY GIVE US

Depending on what you have chosen to share with developers in your device
settings, Apple may give us aggregate usage statistics and crash reports for
the app. That information comes from Apple rather than from the app, reaches
us in a form that does not identify you, and is not combined with anything
else. You control it under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics &
Improvements.
